Product and implementation plan · 24 August 2026

Rewrite the prose. Keep the story.

A plan for a private, reversible chapter-polish feature in Webnovel Archiver. It targets repetitive AI-like habits in fiction without changing plot, character facts, system messages, or the downloaded source.

Recommendation
Ship one chapter at a time, with a verifier.

Keep source HTML immutable. Generate a separate polished version, compare it, then let Reader and TTS use the version the reader chooses.

Coral marks short beats. The problem is not that fragments exist. It is that the same emphasis pattern rarely rests.

01 · Executive verdict

Build “Chapter polish,” not an AI detector

The app should help a reader remove prose habits they dislike. It should not label an author or chapter as AI-written. A detector cannot tell us whether a fragment is intentional, and authorship is irrelevant to the useful action. The reader already knows the chapter is wearing them down.

The first release should rewrite a single downloaded chapter on explicit request. It should produce a separate draft, run a preservation audit, and show source and polished versions before Apply. Applying changes only which local version Reader and TTS resolve. It never overwrites the source chapter file.

User-facing nameChapter polish. “Source” and “Polished” are the two versions.
Default qualityVerified. One rewrite pass, one independent preservation check.
Unit of workOne chapter. Selected-chapter queues come only after the single flow is trusted.
Source policyImmutable, recoverable, and always one tap away.
The most important product limit

A full rewrite can improve rhythm and still damage a story. Models invent transitions, sand down character voices, repair deliberate ambiguity, and change small facts. The preservation verifier and source comparison are core behavior, not premium polish to add later.

What success means, and what it does not

A good run takes a chapter from prose that grates to clean prose that stops announcing itself. It does not produce prose indistinguishable from a talented human author. Comic sensibility and a genuinely distinctive voice are not mechanically injectable, and a rewrite can sand a voice down while cleaning its rhythm. Judge every release on whether the reader stays inside the story, never on an authorship claim.

The example is useful as a stress test. It mixes ordinary narration, first-person comedy, quoted dialogue, emphasis tags, dividers, and large System panels. A rewrite pipeline that handles only plain paragraphs will break this chapter.
02 · What the supplied chapter shows

The emphasis pattern has become the default rhythm

I inspected Chapter 1 of Foxkin of the Night Sky in its rendered Royal Road page. The measurements below describe its HTML paragraphs. They are not an authorship judgment.

187paragraph nodes in the chapter body
91paragraphs of five words or fewer
11clear clusters with three or more clipped fragments
“A pause. A calculation. A prediction.”

This line is not bad because it has three sentences. It is bad in context because the chapter repeats the same move: noun fragments, three-beat judgments, one-line reaction, then another clipped punchline. A rewrite prompt that blindly bans fragments would cause a different failure. It would flatten comedy, tension, and System-fiction pacing into polite mid-length sentences.

  • Stacked near-synonymsSeveral fragments repeat one beat instead of developing it. Merge, cut, or make each beat do different work.
  • Automatic tripletsThree short labels are repeatedly used as a ready-made cadence. Keep a triplet only when the scene earns the ritual or escalation.
  • Reaction restatementThe narration names a feeling, repeats it, then explains it. Preserve the feeling, remove duplicate interpretation.
  • Constant punchline isolationOne-line paragraphs can land jokes. Too many make every thought demand applause.
  • Metaphor after explanationA concrete point is made, then compared, then explained again. Retain the sharpest carrier and trust the reader.
  • Uniform snarkWhen every object receives the same ironic reaction, character voice turns into a template. Keep the best jokes and allow neutral beats.
  • Formatting mixed with proseSystem boxes, dividers, bold labels, and menu choices carry exact game information. They need protected-block handling.
  • Intentional exceptionsA transformation, surprise, or hard scene turn may need fragments. The app needs intensity controls, not one universal ban list.
03 · What to borrow from existing skills and research

A fiction version of Unslop needs a different center of gravity

The provided Unslop skill is a useful starting point for repetition, formulaic contrast, uniform rhythm, filler, and self-audit. Fiction adds constraints that technical prose does not have: viewpoint, tense, narrative distance, character knowledge, dialogue voice, chronology, scene tension, and deliberate rule-breaking.

Borrow

Voice discovery and restraint

The open-source Humanizer skill separates generic cleanup from voice-matching, scales edit intensity to the evidence, and runs a meaning check. Keep that structure. For this app, voice comes from explicit per-novel controls and selected “good” passages, never an invented persona.

Borrow

Pattern-first line editing

Calliope’s line-editor ranks recurring habits instead of painting every sentence red. Its craft criteria include overwriting, rhythm, clarity, voice consistency, abstraction, and word economy. Those are better fiction targets than a generic blacklist.

Borrow

Rhythm judged against intent

Calliope’s prose-rhythm treats sentence length, sound, cadence, and syntax as controls. Low variation is a problem only when it produces an unintended drone. The app should let users pre-clear fragments, repetition, ornate prose, or flat affect when those choices belong to the story.

Borrow

Dialogue as character action

Calliope’s dialogue rubric checks subtext, distinct voices, economy, and what each line does. A rewrite must preserve the speaker, information, and intention even when it changes wording.

Do not copy

A blanket “sound human” prompt

Recent work on LLM style finds model-specific patterns can survive prompts that ask for human-like text. The practical lesson is to specify observable craft targets, use examples, and evaluate the result. “Make this less AI” is too vague to test.

Do not copy

One model grading its own first answer once

Self-Refine supports feedback-and-refinement loops, but a product needs an explicit stop condition. Use one bounded verifier pass and, at most, one repair attempt. Never spend the user’s credits in an open-ended loop.

Evaluation frame

Score three separate outcomes

Text style-transfer research separates style change, content preservation, and naturalness. That is exactly the right frame. A rewrite can sound better while losing facts, or preserve facts while doing nothing useful. The app’s verifier should judge preservation. Human test readers should judge prose improvement and naturalness. Part of style change is countable without a model: fragment clusters, triplet rate, sentence-length variation, and em-dash density all come from the parsed HTML. Those numbers become the deterministic cadence report that runs through the pipeline, the comparison screen, and the release gates.

04 · Product definition

One feature, three edit strengths

Put chapter rewriting in the existing per-novel AI Controls hub and expose a shortcut from the Reader overflow. Store defaults per novel, because the acceptable amount of intervention differs from one story to another.

Low risk

Light edit

Cut obvious repetition, grammar problems, filler, and accidental cadence. Keep dialogue wording and most sentence shapes. Best for prose that is mostly fine.

Recommended default

Balanced polish

Rebuild sentences and paragraphs where needed. Reduce stock fragments, redundant explanation, and constant punchlines. Preserve each scene beat and dialogue intention.

Highest drift risk

Deep rewrite

Allow complete rephrasing of narration and dialogue while locking plot and canon. Always verified. Make the risk and cost clear before generation.

05 · Prompt and generation design

Preservation first, prose second

The prompt should be fiction-specific and ordered by priority. The source chapter is untrusted data. The model rewrites only addressable blocks and returns a structured result that the app can validate before it becomes a draft.

ParseSanitize HTML and classify blocks
ProfilePOV, tense, voice, user controls
RewriteWhole chapter when it fits
RepairOnly if structural validation fails
VerifyCanon, order, speakers, protected text
PreviewSource beside polished draft, with cadence report

The rewrite contract

  • Keep every event, action, decision, revealed fact, relationship, injury, item, number, location, and cause-and-effect link.
  • Keep scene order, point of view, tense, narrator identity, character knowledge, speaker attribution, and dialogue intention.
  • Do not add sensory details, motives, jokes, lore, foreshadowing, explanations, or transitions that the source does not support.
  • Return every input block exactly once and in the same order. Never merge across a scene break.
  • Copy protected blocks exactly. These include System panels, stats, skill descriptions, tables, dividers, and any user-marked passage.
  • Preserve supported emphasis and structural HTML. Return no scripts, styles, event attributes, external media, or new links.

The fiction polish rubric

  • Cut redundant beats. When adjacent fragments say the same thing, keep the sharpest or make each one advance the thought.
  • Use fragments for impact, interruption, shock, or voice. Do not use the same fragment pattern as the default paragraph rhythm.
  • Vary sentence length and syntax according to scene speed. Variation must come from the scene, not random sentence reshuffling.
  • Trust the reader. Remove explanations that merely restate an image, joke, emotion, or action that already landed.
  • Keep character-specific diction and subtext. Do not make everyone cleaner, kinder, wittier, or more articulate.
  • Prefer concrete verbs and specific images already present in the source. Do not invent specificity to make the prose feel alive.
  • Keep deliberate repetition when it escalates, echoes a motif, mimics panic, or establishes ritual. Remove repetition that only fills cadence.
  • Do not normalize genre conventions such as LitRPG boxes, spell names, skill capitalization, or deliberately clipped combat narration.

Structured result

Use OpenRouter structured outputs when the selected model supports them. The response contains an ordered array of { id, html } objects plus a small self-audit. The app validates IDs, order, protected-block hashes, tag allowlists, output size, and completion reason. Unsupported models may use sentinel-delimited text, but the model picker should label that path “basic validation.”

{
  "blocks": [
    { "id": "b0001", "html": "<p>...</p>" },
    { "id": "b0002", "html": "<blockquote>...</blockquote>" }
  ],
  "self_audit": {
    "protected_blocks_unchanged": true,
    "possible_drift": []
  }
}

Long chapter strategy

Prefer one rewrite call when the selected model can hold the prompt, source, and same-length output with headroom. A full-chapter call sees rhythm across scenes and avoids seams. When it cannot fit, split only on HTML block or scene boundaries. Each chunk receives the global edit profile, protected facts, the preceding polished tail, and read-only source context on both sides. It returns only its target block IDs, so overlap cannot duplicate text.

One bounded repair

If the response is truncated, missing block IDs, malformed, or changes protected text, issue one repair call with the validation errors. If it still fails, keep the draft unapplied and show the error. Do not silently accept a partial chapter.

The verifier prompt

The verifier receives original and rewritten block pairs. It does not rewrite prose. It returns typed findings with severity, block IDs, and brief evidence for: missing or added events, changed facts or numbers, speaker or intention drift, POV or tense drift, reordered action, changed System text, invented detail, and missing content. Any blocker prevents Apply. Warnings appear in the comparison screen.

The cadence report

Preservation gets a model verifier; style change gets a countable one. Compute a deterministic before/after report from the sanitized blocks: share of paragraphs at five words or fewer, clusters of three or more clipped fragments, three-beat patterns, sentence-length mean and variation, and em-dash density. Show both columns in the comparison screen, and raise a warning whenever the rewrite reduces fragment clusters while concentrating a new dominant sentence shape: the model swapped templates instead of fixing rhythm. These metrics are pure functions over parsed blocks. They live in the planning layer and get unit tests like every other invariant.

06 · Architecture in this app

Add a content variant, not a replacement chapter

The current app has most of the supporting machinery: a plain OpenRouter client, model picker, exact usage receipts, per-novel AI Controls, process-lifetime cover jobs, atomic file writes, source-preserving AI descriptions, and a shared Reader/TTS chapter read path. The new work should follow those seams.

Current read path
  1. ReaderDocumentPreparer asks the repository for chapter HTML.
  2. AppRepository.readChapter reads Chapter.filePath.
  3. Reader sanitizes and renders it.
  4. TTS separately reads the same source through the repository.
Proposed read path
  1. A ChapterContentResolver asks the rewrite store for an active, complete variant.
  2. If found, it returns polished HTML and its version badge.
  3. Otherwise it returns the immutable source HTML.
  4. Reader, TTS, Copy, and later EPUB call this same resolver.

Storage layout

files/webnovel_archiver/
  novels/<story>/...source chapter html...       # unchanged
  chapter_rewrites/<story>/
    manifest.json                                # atomic, versioned index
    <chapter>/
      applied.html                               # current polished version
      draft.html                                 # complete preview, if any
      job.json                                   # resumable chunk state

Keep rewrite metadata out of Story.chapters. A separate manifest avoids bloating every story JSON, survives source metadata replacement, and keys state by stable story and chapter IDs. It also gives storage one place to clean up drafts, detect stale source hashes, and include applied files in full backups.

Source updates and staleness

Hash the sanitized source HTML before generation. On every read, compare that hash with the record. If a source sync or re-download changes the chapter, keep the polished file but mark it Out of date. Continue showing it if the reader explicitly selected it; do not switch text under them. Offer Compare, Use updated source, and Rewrite again. Never attempt an automatic three-way literary merge.

OpenRouter changes

  • Replace the narrow chatCompletion(..., maxTokens) signature with request options for max output, temperature, structured response format, and provider privacy policy.
  • Extend OpenRouterModel parsing with context length, maximum completion tokens when available, and supported parameters. The chunk planner needs capability data.
  • Add provider: { zdr: true, data_collection: "deny", require_parameters: true } for the privacy-first path. If no endpoint can satisfy it, explain why and let the user decide whether to relax privacy for that run.
  • Record chapter_rewrite, chapter_verify, and chapter_repair receipts under one operation ID. The preview shows their combined cost.

Avoid a premature generic AI job framework

Description generation is activity-scoped, cover generation has two image stages, and chapter rewriting has resumable text chunks plus verification. Reuse conventions and shared request/receipt primitives. Do not force all three into one weak state machine during this feature.

07 · User flow

Make the cost, privacy boundary, and reversibility visible

From Reader
  1. Reader settings overflow adds Polish this chapter.
  2. A bottom sheet shows strength, model, quality mode, privacy rule, calls, and estimated range.
  3. The user confirms Generate polished version.
  4. Reader remains usable while a foreground job runs.
  5. A notification opens the comparison when the draft is ready.
From AI Controls
  1. A new Chapter polish section selects a downloaded chapter.
  2. Per-novel defaults expose edit strength and focused controls.
  3. Existing rewrites show Applied, Draft ready, Out of date, or Failed.
  4. Future batch mode starts here, not in Reader.

Preflight card

  • What leaves the device: this chapter, title, chapter title, limited story metadata, and the selected rewrite profile.
  • Where it goes: OpenRouter and the selected provider. Show whether the request can enforce zero data retention and deny data collection.
  • Estimated work: input/output tokens, number of rewrite chunks, one verifier call or verifier chunks, and a price range based on the model catalog.
  • Rights reminder: for personal reading or text the user has permission to transform. Do not upload or publish another author’s polished chapter.

Comparison screen

On a phone, use Source and Polished tabs with synchronized block navigation. On a Fold inner display, show two panes. Highlight changed paragraphs and verifier warnings rather than a character-level diff, which becomes noisy after a real rewrite. Protected System blocks receive an “Exact copy” badge. A cadence summary sits above the tabs, counting the same habits on both versions, so the reader sees what actually changed without trusting either model. The footer has Apply polished version, Discard, and Regenerate.

Reader state

An applied rewrite adds a small Polished badge beside the chapter counter. Tapping it switches between Source and Polished without leaving Reader. TTS restarts or asks for confirmation if the user changes versions during playback, because chunk indices no longer refer to the same text.

Failure and resume

  • Persist job state after every billed chunk. A killed process resumes missing chunks instead of charging for completed ones again.
  • Never expose partial draft HTML to Reader or TTS.
  • If the source hash changes while the job runs, finish into a quarantined draft and require regeneration against the new source.
  • If the verifier finds a blocker, show the finding and offer one repair. Apply remains disabled.
  • Cancel stops the active OkHttp call and keeps already recorded cost receipts. The user can discard staged files or resume later.
Private does not mean consequence-free. A downloaded chapter sent to an AI provider leaves the device. The feature should default to the strictest OpenRouter routing policy and say when a model cannot satisfy it.
08 · Privacy, rights, and technical safeguards

Constrain the feature to personal, local transformation

Royal Road grants readers a personal-use license and its terms restrict copying, automated access, and other exploitation. The app should not imply that a polished chapter is safe to republish. This plan is a product safeguard, not a legal conclusion.

Rights

Local-only by design

No upload-to-source action, no community sharing, no public rewrite gallery, and no automatic polished EPUB in the first release. If an author polishes their own work and republishes it, Royal Road’s AI-text policy may require disclosure.

Privacy

Strict provider routing

Default to OpenRouter ZDR plus data_collection: deny. OpenRouter says its own prompt logging is opt-in, but model-provider policies differ. Show the selected endpoint’s policy before the first run and whenever it changes.

Prompt injection

Chapter text is data

Delimit serialized source blocks, state that their content cannot change the task, accept only the response schema, and never let returned links or instructions trigger app actions.

HTML

Sanitize twice

Sanitize before classification and sanitize model output through a smaller prose-only allowlist. Reject scripts, styles, iframes, event handlers, new external resources, and unknown attributes.

Durability

Atomic and reversible

Use temp-file, fsync, rename semantics already present in AtomicFileWrites. Applying a rewrite changes a manifest pointer only after the full draft and metadata are durable.

Deletion

One owner for cleanup

Deleting a story removes its applied rewrites, drafts, and job state. Clearing app data clears everything. Full backup includes applied rewrites beside the source chapters and excludes in-flight staging.

Protected block classifier

Start conservatively. Protect tables, repeated border dividers, status/stat panels, list-like System messages, headings, standalone numeric rules, and blocks whose text-to-markup ratio suggests interface content. Let the user long-press any comparison block and mark it “Always keep exact for this novel.” A false positive leaves awkward prose untouched. A false negative may change game rules.

09 · Implementation roadmap

Earn trust before adding scale

Prompt and evaluation spike

No production UI, and no Android code at all: run the spike from a script or the OpenRouter playground. This phase is the go/no-go gate.

  • Build a small corpus from public-domain prose, synthetic LitRPG blocks, and user-authored or permissioned samples, including chapters from the reader’s own problem novels.
  • Compare one-pass, rewrite-plus-verifier, and rewrite-plus-critique-plus-repair using at least three candidate models, one of them frontier-tier. Flash-class models can pass pattern checks while swapping one template rhythm for another, so grade every spike with the cadence report alongside blind preference.
  • If no candidate model produces prose the reader prefers on their own chapters, stop here. Storage, engine, and UI are not worth building against a prompt that cannot clear this bar.
  • Lock prompt v1 only after blind prose preference and preservation grading.

Pure planning and storage

Make the invariants testable before any paid call.

  • Add block parsing/classification, chunk planning, source hashes, typed output validation, record models, and AiChapterRewriteStore.
  • Cover atomic apply/discard, stale-source detection, story deletion, and backup paths with unit tests.

OpenRouter and engine

One chapter, one foreground job.

  • Add request options, structured outputs, provider privacy routing, model capability fields, estimates, grouped receipts, bounded repair, and verifier calls.
  • Persist progress after each completed chunk and recover after process death.

Preview and apply

Trust is built in the comparison.

  • Add Chapter polish controls, preflight estimate, progress, Source/Polished preview, verifier findings, Apply, Discard, and stale status.
  • Add AI_CHAPTER_REWRITE to story operation presentation without blocking unrelated stories.

Reader and TTS integration

One resolver for every consumer.

  • Route Reader, formatted copy, and TTS through ChapterContentResolver.
  • Add the version badge/toggle and safe playback behavior when switching versions.
  • Exercise phone and Fold two-pane comparison layouts on the emulator.

Hardening and rollout

Keep the first public release narrow.

  • Run a permissioned chapter corpus through multiple models, long chapters, mature content, markup-heavy System pages, cancellation, low storage, and stale-source cases.
  • Document privacy, receipts, backup, and prompt-version behavior in docs/ai/.
  • Ship single-chapter Verified mode. Add Fast mode only if its limitations are unmistakable.

Selected-chapter queue

Post-MVP.

  • Let users select downloaded chapters, show a maximum budget, process serially, pause between chapters, and stop when the cap is reached.
  • Add polished EPUB export only after an explicit product and rights review. Keep it opt-in per export.
10 · Test and release gates

Measure preservation and reader preference separately

Deterministic tests
  • All input block IDs returned exactly once, in order.
  • Protected block text and hashes remain byte-for-byte identical after normalization.
  • Unknown tags/attributes are rejected or stripped before preview.
  • Chunk boundaries never split a tag, dialogue block, table row, or scene divider.
  • Truncated and malformed outputs cannot become drafts.
Narrative evals
  • Zero accepted blocker findings for changed facts, numbers, speakers, chronology, or System rules.
  • POV and tense stay fixed unless the profile explicitly changes them.
  • Named entities, pronouns, inventory, injuries, and location state remain consistent.
  • Verifier catches planted subtle errors in a holdout set.
Prose evals
  • Blind A/B readers prefer the polished version for rhythm and clarity on the target corpus.
  • Balanced mode materially reduces fragment clusters and triplet rate on the cadence report without eliminating deliberate fragments.
  • No accepted draft merely trades one dominant rhythm for another; the report flags concentration, not just reduction.
  • Character voices remain distinguishable with speaker tags hidden.
  • No AI detector score is used as a release gate.
Lifecycle and cost
  • Cancel, navigation, rotation, process death, and retry never double-apply or lose billed receipts.
  • The preview’s combined cost matches grouped ledger receipts.
  • Low storage and failed manifest writes preserve the active source/rewrite choice.
  • No more than one automatic repair call occurs per failed response.

Open question for implementation time

The largest unresolved product choice is whether dialogue wording may change in Balanced mode. My recommendation is yes by default, because AI-like dialogue can be part of the problem, but offer “Keep dialogue exact” per novel. The verifier must always preserve speaker, intent, information, and subtext either way.
